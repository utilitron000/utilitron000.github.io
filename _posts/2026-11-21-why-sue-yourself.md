### The Three Card Monte
#### What is three card monte?
The three card monte is one of the most popular confidence games throughout the world, with numerous local variations in every culture across the globe. The game is an evolution of the age-old 'cups and balls' trick that has been performed by illusionists, magicians, sorcerers, and sleight of hand artists across the world since antiquity. Set up in back alleys, often on an unfolded cardboard box (to provide a playing surface that is easy to fold and move), a dealer manipulates 3 cards, usually 2 black face cards and a red Queen. "Follow the red lady," the dealer chants, shuffling and rearranging the cards with a practiced hand. In most games the dealer holds the cash in one of his hands to obscure the sleight of hand being performed. 

If this doesn't already seem like an apt metaphor for the game being played by the plaintiffs of this lawsuit, one must only explore the monte further. Another hallmark of the three card monte is the group of shills keeping watch for police and surrounding the dealer. In variations the shills are either winning easily or losing, but the goal is always the same: to rope a mark and convince them to play.

In the unlikely event that a mark correctly identifies the red Queen, a common move is for a shill to bet higher than the mark on the correct card. The dealer then announces that only the "highest bid plays," and pays out to his shill instead of the mark.
